Abstract

Project Mithila introduces a conceptual space settlement design for an orbital research settlement located within the Jovian lunar system, targeting an ideal lifespan of 50 years. The station serves as a long-term scientific harbor tasked with the systematic exploration of the Jovian and Saturnian moons, with primary focus on the astrobiological assessment of subsurface oceans on Europa, Titan, Callisto and Enceladus. To overcome the extreme constraints of the outer solar system, namely the powerful Jovian magnetosphere and lack of solar radiation, Project Mithila will utilize arrays of radiation-absorbing wings. These wings will be designed to absorb high-energy electrons, and utilize the Lorentz force to5 generate mechanical electricity that can be allocated to specific areas of the settlement. The settlement will involve a Stanford torus that rotates around the cylindrical hull of the settlement, designed to generate artificial gravity via centrifugal force while not impeding human survival. It will also implement an Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS), paired with active electromagnetic deflection and a multi-meter passive mass shield to protect the crew from residual Jovian radiation. This writeup details the structural engineering, rotational dynamics, electrodynamic power generation metrics, and logistical architecture required to operate Project Mithila.

A historical perspective

Mithila: The history behind the name

  • 01 Origins of the ancient kingdom
  • 02 Art as a timeless cultural chronicle
  • 03 Why "Mithila"?

Mithila is an ancient kingdom of profound spiritual and historical legacy in the Indian subcontinent. Celebrated in the epic Ramayana as the realm of King Janaka and the birthplace of Sita, its enduring traditions elegantly bridge the sacred narrative with the natural world.

Art shaped by place

Often called Madhubani art, the ancient city's visual traditions use vibrant colors and geometric motifs to represent relationships between people, ritual, and nature. Project Mithila carries that heritage into a research base focused on exploring the icy moons of the outer Solar System. Its patterns and visual language can give the scientific mission a distinct cultural identity while connecting research into distant oceans with traditions rooted on Earth.

A pattern can be both an image and a record of how its maker sees the world.

Pattern as a system

Project Mithila draws inspiration from the ancient city's art in the research base's visual identity and design. The aim is to balance the clarity and organization scientists need with patterns that honor Mithila's distinctive cultural heritage.

So, why the name?

The name "Mithila" honors the remarkable art and cultural legacy of the ancient city. Bringing that heritage into a research mission among the outer Solar System's moons keeps the project connected to the diversity and creativity of life on Earth.

Mithila — a study of culture, craft, and form. Designed as an evolving cultural archive.
