Mithila is a region and a cultural landscape in the eastern subcontinent. Its visual traditions are not simply decoration: they are ways of recording relationships between people, place, ritual, and the natural world.

Art shaped by place

Often called Madhubani painting, the region's art is known for vivid color, rhythmic line, and surfaces filled with repeated motifs. Artists have traditionally worked with locally available materials and painted on walls, floors, and paper. Styles and practices vary between communities and families; the tradition continues to change as artists make it their own.

A pattern can be both an image and a record of how its maker sees the world.

Pattern as a system

Repetition, symmetry, and carefully nested forms make this art a compelling starting point for thinking about design. These are useful connections to explore—not a claim that historical artists were using modern engineering methods. Looking closely at a motif can raise questions about proportion, structure, material, and the knowledge carried through practice.
